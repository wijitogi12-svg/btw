Fee Monetization: How Sonic Routes Network Fees to Builders

Published: 2026-08-06 Updated: 2026-08-08 Author: Sonic Observer Editorial Team

What happened

Sonic Labs operates a programme called Fee Monetization (often abbreviated FeeM), described in the official documentation as a mechanism that routes up to 90% of the network fees generated by an application back to that application's developers. Instead of all gas fees accruing to validators or being burned, registered apps earn a share of the fees their own users generate on the Sonic network.

Why it matters

Most blockchains give application developers no direct share of the network fees their products create; monetisation usually has to come from separate app-level fees or token launches. Fee Monetization changes that default by making transaction volume itself a revenue stream for builders. Conceptually it resembles ad-revenue sharing on web platforms: the network gives up part of its fee income to attract and retain the applications that generate activity in the first place.

Risks and caveats

The model's incentives cut both ways. Revenue that scales with transaction count could, in theory, encourage applications optimised for volume rather than usefulness, and the programme's parameters — the percentage shared, eligibility rules, payout mechanics — are defined by Sonic Labs and can change. Developers considering the programme should read the current terms in the official documentation rather than relying on summaries, including this one. Participation also does not guarantee any particular level of income, since earnings depend entirely on real usage.

Market context

Developer incentives have become a key battleground among layer-1 and layer-2 networks, alongside grants programmes and ecosystem funds. Sonic pairs Fee Monetization with an Innovator Fund of up to 200 million S for ecosystem projects. Whether fee-sharing proves more durable than one-off grants is an open question the wider industry is watching.
