Official links: verifying channels before you transact

Phishing that imitates official channels remains one of the most effective attacks in this sector. Verifying a link is a security step, not an administrative one.

All topics
Published8 August 2026
Updated8 August 2026
Author / EditorialBitway Brief Editorial Desk
SectionReference

The cited channels

Secondary sources consistently cite a main website and a documentation subdomain, an application subdomain for the Earn product, a public code organisation, and social channels on X, Telegram and Discord. A token contract on BNB Chain is cited and can be cross-checked on a public explorer.

We are deliberately not reproducing a long list of URLs here. Any list on a third-party site — including this one — can go stale or be manipulated, and reproducing links gives them an authority they have not earned.

How to verify independently

Reach official channels through at least two independent paths: for example, a link published in the documentation and a link published from a long-standing verified social account, then confirm they agree. Check that a social account's history predates the current campaign; impersonation accounts are usually new.

For contract addresses, compare the documentation against a block explorer and a major data aggregator. Never take an address from a direct message, a comment, a screenshot, or a search advertisement — paid search results imitating official sites are a routine attack.

Red flags worth remembering

No legitimate support channel asks for a seed phrase or private key, ever. Offers that require an immediate deposit, promise a fixed or guaranteed return, or pressure users with a countdown are hallmarks of fraud. Airdrop claim pages requesting unlimited token approvals should be treated as hostile until proven otherwise.
