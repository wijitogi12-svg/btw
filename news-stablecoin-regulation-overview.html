Stablecoin Regulation: Where the Rules Are Heading and Why Reserves Matter

What happened

Regulators across major jurisdictions — including the EU, the UK, the UAE and several Asian financial centres — have been introducing or finalising dedicated stablecoin frameworks. Common themes include full reserve backing in high-quality liquid assets, regular independent attestations, and licensing requirements for issuers.

Why it matters

Stablecoins are the settlement layer for much of the digital-asset economy, and they increasingly interact with traditional payments. Clear reserve and redemption rules reduce the risk that a widely used token trades below its peg during market stress, an event that has historically triggered broader sell-offs.

Risks and open questions

Fragmented rules across jurisdictions create compliance complexity for global issuers and may push activity toward less regulated venues. There are also open questions about how offshore-issued stablecoins will be treated in strict regimes, and whether smaller issuers can bear the cost of licensing and attestation requirements.

Market context

The regulatory direction of travel is toward treating large stablecoin issuers more like payment institutions or narrow banks. For users, the practical takeaway is that reserve transparency and the issuer’s regulatory status are becoming primary quality signals when evaluating any stablecoin.
